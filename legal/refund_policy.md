## Subscription fees
Subscription fees pay for the selected service/access period and contribute to server hosting, database operation, security, maintenance, support and other services that keep the app working. A change-of-mind request after the agreed service has begun is assessed against the service already supplied, the purchase disclosures and applicable law. Access having started does not by itself remove a cooling-off right. Cancellation of renewal stops future charges in line with disclosed renewal terms; it does not automatically reverse an already supplied period. These fees are not an indefinite personal-data-storage fee and do not override privacy/deletion obligations.

## Mandatory rights take precedence
This policy does not exclude applicable cooling-off, fixed-term cancellation, unfair-term, non-supply, defective-service, duplicate/unauthorised-payment or other statutory remedies. Where law requires a refund, the company will honour it. Any early-start consent must be separate, informed and recorded; a general checkbox is not assumed to remove every cooling-off right.

## Tutor booking fees: at least 24 hours' notice
A paid booking cancelled at least 24 hours before its scheduled start is eligible for a full booking-fee refund. The server measures the exact interval using the session's South African time; cancellation exactly 24 hours beforehand qualifies. Submit the in-app cancellation and write a message informing the tutor that you are cancelling, allowing them to rearrange their day. The message and cancellation timestamp are recorded. A queued or failed email does not invalidate an otherwise timely recorded cancellation or make the learner responsible for an email-provider failure.

## Later cancellations, tutor cancellation and non-delivery
For later cancellations and no-shows, any cancellation charge must be fair, disclosed and justified in the circumstances, including notice and reasonable opportunities to rebook. There is no automatic entitlement to keep the entire booking price. No blanket forfeiture is imposed. Death or hospitalisation affecting the booked beneficiary receives the protection required by law. Do not request detailed medical records through public messages; use a secure minimal-evidence review. A tutor/company cancellation or failure to deliver a booked service is referred for full booking-fee refund when payment was received. Unpaid bookings have no money to return.

## Processing and loss prevention
Refund eligibility is not proof of completed repayment. Refund only the amount actually collected, once, against the original payment/booking reference and normally through the original payment method where practicable. Do not refund subscription revenue as if it were a booking payment. Do not mark a booking refunded or release an associated tutor payout until settlement is verified. Keep an audit trail and reconcile chargebacks to avoid duplicate reimbursement. Do not deduct undisclosed penalties or use processing costs to defeat a mandatory full refund. Contact support with your reference; provider timelines and applicable statutory deadlines will be communicated during review.

## Cooling-off and fixed-term rights
Where ECTA section 44 applies to an electronic service transaction, the consumer generally has seven days after concluding the agreement to cancel without reason or penalty, subject to statutory exclusions. Refunds due under that provision must be made within 30 days of cancellation. A service started with the consumer's consent within the cooling-off period can fall within an exception; do not assume that merely logging in or ticking general terms establishes informed early-start consent. Other products and circumstances must be assessed under the applicable rules.

Where CPA section 14 applies to a fixed-term agreement, a consumer may cancel on 20 business days' recorded notice, subject to a reasonable cancellation charge where permitted. Applicable expiry/renewal notices and rights must be honoured. Direct-marketing cooling-off rights may apply under CPA section 16; they do not simply stack on ECTA-covered transactions. If more favourable commercial terms were promised, honour them. These statutory rights do not require permission from a tutor.

## Non-delivery, quality and technical interruption
For a tutor cancellation or wholly undelivered paid lesson, offer a full booking-fee refund; rescheduling is the customer's choice. Where delivery was partial or materially deficient, assess a reasonable remedy, including correction or an appropriate refund, subject to mandatory rights. Do not treat a brief outage as proof that a whole service was supplied or automatically charge a learner for a failure attributable to the platform. Learner-side connectivity problems require a fair review of what happened and any disclosed reasonable charge.

## Giving notice and following a refund
Use the available cancellation action and a short written notice to the tutor. If the app fails, email support with the booking reference and scheduled date/time. Support must record the notice and consider the evidence of when it was received. A tutor's failure to acknowledge, or a failed system email, does not defeat a valid timely cancellation. The 24-hour promise is a commercial full-refund benefit; later notice does not eliminate statutory protection.

Support will communicate the decision, calculation, original-payment reference, expected processing time and any lawful reason for refusal. Complain if a refund is delayed; provider processing does not excuse a statutory deadline. A full-refund entitlement must not be reduced by an undisclosed platform, administration or payment fee. Credits cannot replace a money refund without your agreement where a money refund is due. Refund only once, taking account of any chargeback already paid.

## Separation from payment activation
This policy governs decisions when money has actually been collected; it does not activate a payment gateway, promise an unverified automated refund process, or authorise a transfer. Until the relevant functionality is working, do not sell a service on the promise that an unavailable payment/refund mechanism exists.
