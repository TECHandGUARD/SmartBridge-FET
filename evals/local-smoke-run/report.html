Resistance is a measure of how much a resistor opposes the flow of electric current. To find the resistance, you can use Ohm's Law, which states that $ V = IR $, where $ V $ is voltage, $ I $ is current, and $ R $ is resistance.

Given:
- Voltage $ V = 12 \, \text{V} $
- Current $ I = 0.5 \, \text{A} $

Using Ohm's Law:
$$ R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{12 \, \text{V}}{0.5 \, \text{A}} = 24 \, \Omega $$

The units of resistance are ohms ($\Omega$).

So, the resistance is **24 ohms**. This means the resistor has a resistance of 24 ohms, which is a measure of its opposition to current flow.

$$

The resultant force is different from any single applied force because **resultant force** accounts for the **vector sum** of all forces acting on an object. In summary, the resultant force is the total effect of all forces acting on the object, and it is different from a single applied force because it considers the vector nature of forces.
