# SmartBridge FET | Grade 12 Life Sciences — Human Reproduction

**Curriculum:** Grade 12 Life Sciences — Human reproduction (check final sequencing against the current DBE ATP and CAPS).

## Learning outcomes
By the end, a learner should be able to: identify and give functions of reproductive structures; outline gametogenesis; describe menstrual/ovarian cycles and hormone feedback; distinguish fertilisation, implantation and gestation; explain placenta functions and childbirth.

## 1. Male reproductive anatomy
**Structures and functions:** Testes — produce sperm and testosterone; scrotum — holds testes at a temperature generally below core body temperature; epididymis — sperm maturation/storage; vas deferens (sperm duct) — transports sperm; seminal vesicles and prostate — add substances to seminal fluid; urethra — channel for semen (and urine at different times); penis — delivers semen.
**Diagram A specification:** Original, medically accurate sagittal schematic of male reproductive system; label bladder, testes, scrotum, epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle, prostate, urethra and penis. Use leader lines checked by an educator; don't confuse vas deferens with ureter.

## 2. Female reproductive anatomy
**Structures and functions:** Ovaries — produce ova and secrete oestrogen and progesterone; fallopian tubes (oviducts) — usually the site of fertilisation; uterus — site of implantation and fetal development; endometrium — uterine lining that changes during the menstrual cycle; cervix — narrow lower opening of uterus; vagina — receives sperm and acts as birth canal.
**Diagram B specification:** Anterior schematic showing ovaries, fimbriae, fallopian tubes, uterus, endometrium, cervix, vagina. Labels must point unambiguously to the correct structures. Do not show fertilisation occurring inside the uterus.

## 3. Gametogenesis
**Spermatogenesis:** In seminiferous tubules, diploid precursor cells produce haploid sperm through meiosis and further maturation. One primary spermatocyte can form four functional sperm after meiosis and differentiation.
**Oogenesis:** Begins before birth; normally one functional ovum forms from each primary oocyte, with polar bodies. The secondary oocyte is released at ovulation; meiosis II completes only if fertilisation occurs.
**Diagram C specification:** Comparison flowcharts of spermatogenesis and oogenesis, marking diploid (2n) versus haploid (n), meiosis I/II and unequal cytokinesis in oogenesis. Avoid implying that four functional ova form.

## 4. Menstrual cycle and hormones
**Typical textbook 28-day illustration only:** Menstruation is often drawn around days 1–5; ovulation near day 14; actual cycle lengths and timing vary. FSH stimulates follicular development; developing follicles secrete oestrogen; sustained high oestrogen contributes to the LH surge; LH triggers ovulation; the corpus luteum produces progesterone and oestrogen to maintain the endometrium. If implantation does not occur, corpus luteum regression lowers progesterone and oestrogen, contributing to menstruation.
**Diagram D specification:** Aligned plots for FSH, LH, oestrogen, progesterone and a separate endometrial thickness panel; label phases and show day 14 as *illustrative*, not universal. Do not depict oestrogen and progesterone peaking at identical times or FSH as the direct trigger of ovulation.

## 5. Fertilisation, implantation, gestation, birth
1. **Fertilisation:** Typically takes place in the fallopian tube where sperm and secondary oocyte fuse to form a diploid zygote.
2. **Early development:** Cleavage produces successive cell divisions; a blastocyst forms and reaches the uterus.
3. **Implantation:** The blastocyst embeds in the uterine endometrium, usually several days after fertilisation.
4. **Placenta:** Facilitates exchange of oxygen, nutrients and wastes between maternal and fetal blood without their blood normally mixing directly; also secretes hormones. The umbilical cord links fetus and placenta.
5. **Childbirth:** Cervical dilation, delivery of baby, then delivery of placenta. Oxytocin contributes to strong uterine contractions through positive feedback.
**Diagram E specification:** Five separate simple process panels: fertilisation in oviduct → cleavage → blastocyst → implantation in endometrium → placenta/umbilical cord, with directional arrows. Keep developmental stages anatomically positioned correctly.

## Assessment practice — 20 marks
1. State two functions of the testes. (2) **Memo:** sperm production (1); testosterone production (1).
2. Name the typical site of fertilisation and the site of implantation. (2) **Memo:** fallopian tube/oviduct (1); uterine endometrium (1).
3. Explain why the LH surge is important. (2) **Memo:** triggers ovulation (1), release of secondary oocyte from mature follicle (1).
4. Differentiate the products of spermatogenesis and oogenesis in terms of functional gametes. (4) **Memo:** four functional haploid sperm per primary spermatocyte (2); usually one functional ovum plus polar bodies per primary oocyte (2).
5. Describe how progesterone changes when pregnancy does not occur, and give its effect on the uterine lining. (3) **Memo:** corpus luteum degenerates (1); progesterone falls (1); endometrium breaks down/menstruation follows (1).
6. State three functions of the placenta. (3) **Memo:** exchange oxygen (1), nutrients (1), wastes (1); credit hormone secretion when appropriately justified.
7. Explain two ways the male and female reproductive systems contribute to successful fertilisation. (4) **Memo:** male: produces and transports viable sperm (2); female: produces secondary oocyte and permits meeting of gametes in oviduct (2).
