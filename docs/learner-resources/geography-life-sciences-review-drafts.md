## Geography: Pollution domes and temperature inversions
**Provisional placement:** Geography · Grade 12 · Atmospheric processes (confirm against current CAPS teaching plan).

### Learner outcomes
- Describe how pollutants can accumulate in a city during stable atmospheric conditions.
- Explain the relationship between a temperature inversion, limited vertical mixing, and air quality.
- Interpret a conceptual cross-section without claiming that every pollution event is caused by an inversion.

### Understand
A **pollution dome** describes a build-up of air pollution above and around an urban area when pollutants are produced faster than they disperse. Traffic, industry and other combustion sources can contribute particulate matter and gases. Under normal conditions, warmer air near the surface can rise and mix with surrounding air. During a **temperature inversion**, a layer of warmer air lies above cooler air near the ground; this stable structure makes vertical mixing more difficult and can trap pollutants near breathing level. Light winds and surrounding topography may worsen the build-up. A pollution dome is a conceptual model, not a solid physical dome.

### Diagram specification — original high-resolution vector required
Show a city cross-section with traffic and industrial emission sources, cool near-surface air containing pollutants, a clearly labelled warmer inversion layer above, and restrained upward arrows indicating limited vertical mixing. Include a legend that separates *pollutant emissions* from *air movement*. Avoid depicting inversion as an impermeable wall. Include alt text describing the diagram, and ensure labels remain legible on a small phone.

**Caption:** “Stable air and a temperature inversion can reduce dispersion, causing pollutants to accumulate near the ground.”

### South African context
On a calm winter morning, emissions from roads and household fuel use may build up over an urban area. The severity depends on local weather, emission levels, landform and other factors; do not claim that all South African cities experience identical conditions.

### Worked example
**Question:** Why can a temperature inversion worsen air quality on a windless morning?
**Reasoning:** (1) Cooler near-surface air stays beneath a warmer layer. (2) Reduced vertical mixing allows emitted pollutants to remain concentrated near ground level. (3) People may experience greater exposure until conditions change.

### Practice with answer guide
1. **Define a temperature inversion.** — A reversal of the usual lower-atmosphere temperature pattern in which warmer air overlies cooler air near the ground.
2. **Name two possible urban pollution sources.** — Vehicle exhaust and industrial emissions (other appropriate sources accepted).
3. **Explain one way stronger winds may change the pollution dome.** — They may disperse and dilute pollutants, depending on prevailing conditions.
4. **Distinguish the inversion layer from the pollution dome.** — The inversion is a temperature structure; the pollution dome is accumulated urban pollution.

## Life Sciences: The human heart and blood flow
**Provisional placement:** Life Sciences · Grades 11–12 **pending precise CAPS grade verification**.

### Learner outcomes
- Identify the four heart chambers and major blood vessels.
- Trace the route of blood through pulmonary and systemic circulation.
- Explain why valves are important.

### Understand
The heart is a muscular pump divided into right and left sides. The **right atrium** receives deoxygenated blood from the body through the **superior and inferior vena cava**. Blood passes through the **tricuspid valve** into the **right ventricle**, which pumps it through the **pulmonary valve** and **pulmonary arteries** to the lungs. Oxygenated blood returns via the **pulmonary veins** to the **left atrium**, passes through the **mitral (bicuspid) valve** to the **left ventricle**, and leaves through the **aortic valve** and **aorta** for the body. Valves help maintain one-way blood flow. In a front-view diagram, the heart's anatomical right is commonly on the viewer's left.

### Diagram specification — original high-resolution vector required
Depict all four chambers and label: superior/inferior vena cava, right atrium, tricuspid valve, right ventricle, pulmonary valve, pulmonary artery, pulmonary veins, left atrium, mitral valve, left ventricle, aortic valve, and aorta. Direction arrows must follow the route in the paragraph above; check the anatomical left/right convention. Use **both** written oxygenation labels and differentiated arrows/patterns so understanding does not depend on red/blue colour alone. Include alt text. Do not put educational labels solely inside a generative image without human verification.

**Caption:** “The right side pumps blood to the lungs; the left side pumps oxygenated blood to the body.”

### Worked example
**Question:** Trace blood returning from the body until it leaves the heart for the body again.
**Answer:** Venae cavae → right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle → pulmonary valve → pulmonary arteries → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium → mitral valve → left ventricle → aortic valve → aorta.

### Practice with answer guide
1. **Which chamber pumps blood to the lungs?** — Right ventricle.
2. **Which blood vessels bring oxygenated blood from the lungs?** — Pulmonary veins.
3. **Why are valves important?** — They prevent reverse blood flow.
4. **Why is the left ventricular wall relatively thick?** — It pumps blood at higher pressure through systemic circulation.
5. **Are all arteries oxygenated? Explain.** — No. The pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood from the heart to the lungs; arteries are defined by direction *away* from the heart.
